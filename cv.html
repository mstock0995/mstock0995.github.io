Research

  1. Aveklouris, A., DeValve, L., Stock, M., and Ward, A. (2025). “Matching Impatient and Heterogeneous Demand and Supply.” Operations Research, 73(3), 1637–1658.

    Matching platforms must decide which customers and suppliers to match before they lose patience and leave. We develop matching policies that approach optimal performance as arrival rates grow, and identify conditions under which a fixed ranking of matches is sufficient. We also show that the full distribution of patience times, not only its mean, matters for choosing these priorities: two populations with the same average patience can require different priorities.

  2. Stock, M., and DeValve, L. (2026). “Joint Ordering, Allocation, and Fulfillment for Capacitated Multiechelon Batch Inventory Networks.” Major Revision, Operations Research.

    We develop an approximation framework for joint truck ordering, pallet inventory placement, and online fulfillment in a capacitated two-echelon network. Its main technical component is a constant-factor approximation algorithm for the SAA problem based on LP rounding, clustering, and reallocation. Combined with an online fulfillment policy, the framework has a performance guarantee relative to the optimal policy. Numerical experiments show that the rounding performs substantially better than its worst-case guarantee and can outperform simple rounding by more than 60%.

  3. Stock, M., and DeValve, L. (2026). “Data-driven newsvendor for fulfillment networks.” Manuscript available upon request.

    We establish finite-sample guarantees for learning inventory placements in fulfillment networks from historical demand. Under mild network-cost conditions, sample average approximation achieves near-optimal expected cost with high probability, requiring only finite mean demand and allowing arbitrary dependence across locations. Our bounds quantify how data requirements depend on the number of warehouses, network costs, and desired accuracy.

  4. Han, X.Y., DeValve, L., and Stock, M. (2026). “Prefix-sharing KV-cache models for LLM inference.” Work in progress.

    We investigate stochastic models linking prefix sharing to memory requirements and cache reuse in LLM inference. Our empirical work distinguishes substantial reuse potential within conversations from weaker sharing across conversations.

  5. Sotelo, R., Gatti, L., Machin, M., Stock, M., Méndez, I., Fernández-Ojeda, J., Gibert, D., and Orihuela, J.-D. (2021). “Determination of the Optimal Distribution for Loading Cargo Vehicles Using the IBM Qiskit VQE Algorithm.” 2021 IEEE URUCON, 435–439.
