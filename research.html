Matching Impatient and Heterogeneous Demand and Supply

Angelos Aveklouris, Levi DeValve, Maximiliano Stock, Amy Ward

Operations Research 73(3), 1637–1658

Matching platforms must decide which customers and suppliers to match before they lose patience and leave. We develop matching policies that approach optimal performance as arrival rates grow, and identify conditions under which a fixed ranking of matches is sufficient. We also show that the full distribution of patience times, not only its mean, matters for choosing these priorities: two populations with the same average patience can require different priorities.

Joint Ordering, Allocation, and Fulfillment for Capacitated Multiechelon Batch Inventory Networks

Maximiliano Stock, Levi DeValve

Job market paper. Major revision, Operations Research

How should a firm coordinate inventory decisions when it orders in truckloads, stores inventory in pallets, and fulfills demand in units? We develop an approximation framework for these decisions in a capacitated two-echelon network. A new clustering technique allows us to round fractional inventory into whole pallets while respecting warehouse capacities and controlling fulfillment costs. We establish performance guarantees and quantify how sampling demand affects the resulting decisions.

Data-Driven Newsvendor for Fulfillment Networks

Maximiliano Stock, Levi DeValve

Working paper

How much demand data does a firm need to choose a good inventory placement across a fulfillment network? We use the structure of the problem to study cost differences between placements directly, even when their individual costs are difficult to estimate. This allows us to bound how many demand observations are sufficient for a decision chosen from the data to have expected cost close to optimal. The guarantee requires only finite mean demand, allowing distributions with infinite variance.

Prefix-sharing KV-cache models for LLM inference

with Levi DeValve and X.Y. Han

Work in progress.

Memory requirements for serving LLMs depend not only on how many requests are being processed, but also on what those requests share. When requests begin with identical tokens, their shared KV-cache entries can be stored once. We study how this sharing translates into memory requirements, and derive simple bounds based on the frequencies of common and rare prefixes. Experiments with conversation and benchmark data show why sharing measured on prompts alone can overstate the sharing available once generated outputs are included.

Accounting, Legal Institutions, and the Adoption of Good Management Practices

Pablo Casas-Arce, F. Asís Martínez-Jerez, Maximiliano Stock

Under review, Contemporary Accounting Research. Work done before the PhD.

Using World Management Survey data from 35 countries, we study how firms’ management practices, such as performance monitoring, targets, and incentives, vary with the quality of accounting information and the strength of legal institutions.
